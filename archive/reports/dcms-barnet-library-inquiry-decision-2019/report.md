# Local inquiry into library provision in Barnet

**1.** The Secretary of State acknowledges the level of local interest in relation to the library service changes agreed by Barnet Council (BC) and the particular criticisms and concerns raised about the implications and impact of the changes to an important and highly regarded local service. He fully recognises the value and importance of public libraries in supporting local people and communities. In addition to providing access to books and literature, they can also offer a variety of services that help people of all ages to help themselves and improve their opportunities. They can bring people together, and provide practical support and guidance. The Secretary of State recognises that where changes to library service provision are proposed they may not always satisfy the wishes of all local residents.

**2.** The Secretary of State has considered whether to intervene by directing an inquiry under the Public Libraries and Museums Act 1964 ("1964 Act") into the changes in the library provision in Barnet. He has decided not to direct a local inquiry for the reasons set out below.

**3.** On 18 December 2017, the then Secretary of State decided that she was not minded to direct an inquiry under the 1964 Act ("the minded to letter"), but invited further representations before taking a final decision. A total of twenty-five representations were received from library users or interested groups or persons.

## Principles

**4.** The Secretary of State has considered the duty of a local authority to provide a comprehensive and efficient library service under section 7 of the 1964 Act. What constitutes a comprehensive and efficient service is a question involving a significant element of judgement. Those judgements are, in the first instance, for the local authority to make. It has in-depth knowledge of local conditions and needs and has direct democratic accountability to the local population. This is a significant factor. The Secretary of State's view is that decisions about local issues should ordinarily be taken by democratically elected local representatives accountable to local voters.

<!-- PAGE: 2 -->

**5.** In determining his opinion the Secretary of State noted the previous views in High Court cases set out in the minded to letter of 18 December 2017, in particular the case of Draper v Lincolnshire County Council [2014] EWHC 2388 (Admin), Ouseley J in Bailey v London Borough of Brent [2011] EWHC 2572 (Admin) and R (Green) v Gloucestershire City Council [2011] EWHC 2687 (Admin).

**6.** The duty of the Secretary of State is one of superintendence of the duty placed on local authorities. A wide range of approaches are open to the local authority when deciding how to provide a comprehensive and efficient library service. It is not the function of the Secretary of State to substitute his opinion for that of the democratically accountable local authority. The question which the Secretary of State must consider is whether the BC library service, as revised following changes agreed by the Council on 4 April 2016, is comprehensive and efficient.

**7.** The Secretary of State seeks to promote and secure the proper discharge of the statutory duties on local authorities. He has power to direct a local inquiry. His approach in deciding whether to intervene to direct an inquiry has been to ask himself whether, having regard to the duties on him and the local authority, there is good reason in all the circumstances for him to direct an inquiry at the present time.

**8.** In taking that decision, the Secretary of State has given consideration to a number of factors. They include:

- Whether there is any serious doubt or uncertainty as to whether BC is (or may cease to be) complying with its legal obligation to provide a comprehensive and efficient library service.
- Whether BC appears to be acting in a careless or unreasonable way.
- Whether the decision is or may be outside the proper bounds of BC's discretion, such as a capricious decision to stop serving a particularly vulnerable group in the local community.
- Whether BC appears to have failed to consult affected individuals or to carry out significant research into the effects of its proposals.
- Whether BC has failed to explain, analyse or properly justify its proposals.
- Whether the local proposals are likely to lead to a breach of national library policy.
- The advantages of local decision making by expert and democratically accountable local representatives.
- Whether there is any further good reason why a local inquiry should be ordered.

## Library changes in Barnet

**9.** The library changes in Barnet approved by BC on 6 April 2016 involved the retention of all 14 libraries and mobile library and the introduction of a four tier library model, comprising: four Core Plus libraries (Chipping Barnet, Edgware, Finchley Church End and Hendon); six Core libraries; and four Partnership libraries (operated in partnership with voluntary and community groups). In addition, the Council agreed to maintain the existing Schools Library Service, mobile and home library service, the digital library, Local Studies and Archives service and the Early Years' service, as well as support for reading groups, Baby Rhyme Time and other activities. To deliver this library offer within the reduced funding envelope, BC introduced two main changes.

**10.** The first was to reduce the total number of staff and to introduce a revised schedule of staffed opening hours at the 10 Core and Core Plus libraries, providing staff on-site to assist users, undertake library duties, deliver activities and work with local partners and with some volunteer support. This change has resulted in the reduction in the total number of staffed weekly hours at Core and Core Plus libraries, from 634.5 to 187 staffed hours. The second was to introduce technology enabled opening (self-service opening) at Core and Core Plus libraries to provide access to libraries without staff on-site. By enabling users to access the library outside of staffed hours, the total opening hours across BC's static

<!-- PAGE: 3 -->

library network were intended to increase from 634.5 to 904 weekly hours (as agreed by BC in April 2016). In addition to the four Partnership libraries, this would mean that 10 libraries would be operating with extended opening hours offering increased early morning and evening access and access on more days of the week.

**11.** Other changes introduced by BC included the opening of two new replacement library buildings (at Church End and Colindale) and the refurbishment and reconfiguration of the majority of the remaining static libraries.

**12.** BC consider that this level of library provision enables it to provide an efficient and comprehensive service, while meeting the council's wider savings challenge.

## Further representations

**13.** A total of 25 further representations were received in response to the minded to letter.

**14.** A number of the points made in the representations raise issues already considered by the Secretary of State and where nothing substantially new is added. It is not possible, or necessary, in this letter to respond to every point made, nor to each and every representation (as it appears to the Secretary of State). However, each and every representation has been carefully considered.

## Decision

**15.** The Secretary of State's duty under the 1964 Act is one of superintendence. It is not the function of the Secretary of State to substitute his opinion for that of the democratically accountable local authority in how it discharges its primary duty. Not every alteration in library provision will justify a costly local inquiry and the uncertainty that it brings. In this case, the Secretary of State's view is that an inquiry is not appropriate.

**16.** The specific question which the Secretary of State must consider is whether the statutory library service provision, following implementation of the agreed changes, comprising 10 Core and Core Plus libraries, with a combined staffed and technology enabled opening hours of 647.5 hours per week, four Partnership libraries, as well as the continuation of the mobile and home library service, the digital library service, Local Studies and Archives service, the Schools Library Service, the Early Years' service, as well as support for adults, children and teenagers, including reading groups, Baby Rhyme Time and other activities is comprehensive and efficient.

**17.** The Secretary of State has considered the additional representations in light of the factors referred to on page 2 of this letter and has found the factors explored below, which were previously explored, to be of particular relevance to this matter.

- Whether the Council appears to be acting in a careless or unreasonable way
- Whether the decision is or may be outside the proper bounds of the Council's discretion, such as a capricious decision to stop serving a particularly vulnerable group in the local community.

<!-- PAGE: 4 -->

**18.** The additional representations imply that BC has failed to perform its continuing duty under equalities legislation, by failing to review and update the Equality Impact Assessment (EIA) to capture actual impacts, and failing to undertake risk assessments of technology enabled opening for disabled users. They claim that BC is in breach of its duties under the Equality Act 2010. They also comment that BC has failed to put in place mitigations to lessen the impact upon certain groups of users (e.g. young people, older people, users with disabilities and carers) who, it is claimed, are no longer able to use the library service.

**19.** The representations also suggest that the Secretary of State does not appear to have sought confirmation that BC has in fact carried out the promised reviews and mitigations.

**20.** BC states that it has paid due regard to its obligations under equalities legislation and continues to do so in the implementation of the library changes. It commented that the EIA was reviewed on a regular basis prior to the decision of 4 April 2016, and that it has been subject to subsequent reviews to help determine the implementation and mitigation approach. BC confirmed that the EIA was reviewed in January 2017 to capture any change in impact on protected groups that may arise from proposed changes to the pattern of staffed and technology enabled opening hours (following local consultation on the proposed pattern of opening hours).

**21.** BC further states that the EIA is reviewed annually. It also notes that it developed a 100-day review of the service which was considered by the Children, Education, Libraries and Safeguarding (CELS) Committee in September 2017 and that an Annual Update on the library service was considered by the CELS Committee in June 2018. It states that both reports provided detail of the on-going mitigations to address the impacts identified in the EIA. In addition, BC commented that it undertook a formal review of the library service in the summer of 2018 and that this was reported to the Policy and Resources Committee in December 2018. The report set out findings from the consultation, as well as customer satisfaction and other performance data and identified areas for further development, including staffed and self-service opening hours, Partnership libraries and children and young people. BC notes that this was followed by a subsequent report to the CELS Committee in March 2019. BC also confirmed that the next review of the full EIA is due to take place in 2019.

**22.** BC confirmed that the most recent EIA highlighted that changes to staffed opening hours may disproportionately impact some protected characteristic groups, and that a number of actions were agreed to mitigate the impact of the changes upon these groups. BC also confirmed that a full risk assessment of self-service opening at Core and Core Plus libraries was completed before technology enabled opening went live.

### Secretary of State's view

**23.** The Secretary of State notes that BC has indicated that it keeps under review the impact of the changes upon users, including those with protected characteristics. He also notes BC's intention to undertake and complete a formal and independent review of the library service by 2020. The Secretary of State expects that BC will ensure that this review involves a thorough and detailed assessment of the library changes, which should include the impact upon users with protected characteristics and that BC takes appropriate action to address any negative impacts identified in this process.

<!-- PAGE: 5 -->

**24.** In respect of the criticism that the EIA has not been reviewed to date, the Secretary of State is satisfied that BC has reviewed the EIA at appropriate stages during the development and implementation of the library changes, that BC has in place plans to undertake a further review in 2019 and that it has continued to review and assess the impact of the changes through the formal reviews of the library service in 2017 and 2018.

**25.** The Secretary of State notes the criticism that he does not appear to have sought confirmation that BC has carried out the promised reviews and mitigations. As explained in the minded to letter, the Secretary of State's role is one of superintendence, and it is not the function of the Secretary of State to duplicate the work of the democratically accountable local authority, or to question every detail of the work undertaken by it. The Secretary of State considered the information provided by the complainant and BC, which provided details of the work that had been, and was being, taken forward. The Secretary of State was satisfied that this demonstrated that BC had given appropriate consideration to its statutory duty given these factors.

**26.** The Secretary of State's view is that it is for BC, as the democratically accountable body of local representatives, to ensure that it has in place appropriate processes and governance structures to enable robust and well-considered decisions to be taken, including in accordance with its obligations under the Equalities Act 2010. The Secretary of State's role is one of superintendence under the 1964 Act and, in this regard, he is satisfied that BC has acted reasonably in carrying out its on-going equality obligations and that BC has and continues to review and update the EIA as appropriate.

- Whether there is any serious doubt or uncertainty as to whether the Council is (or may cease to be) complying with its legal obligation to provide a comprehensive and efficient library service.
- Whether BC has failed to explain, analyse or properly justify its proposals

**27.** The further representations raised comment on issues relating to library usage, opening hours, young people, older people and people with disabilities, suggesting there are doubts that BC's library service is comprehensive and efficient.

## Registration, usage and access issues

**28.** The representations state that there has been a low take up of people registering for self-service opening, because of the intrusive and off-putting nature of the registration process and the lack of communication of self-service opening and how to register. They also state that this has contributed to a decline in library visits and book issues.

**29.** BC commented that as at 31 March 2018, 16,843 users had registered for technology enabled opening and that this number had increased to 22,230 users by the end of December 2018 (representing 11% of active library users), with a target of 23,000 users by the end of March 2019. It also states that 53% of users who have attended a self-service opening induction have gone on to use a library during technology enabled opening.

**30.** BC stated that library users are able to register for technology enabled opening in person at staffed sessions or by attending an induction session. BC commented that posters and leaflets advertising self-service opening are available at all libraries. It confirmed that information is also displayed on the Council's website, the library pin-boards and the Self-Service Opening (SSO) noticeboard and assistance is also available from security guards. BC also

<!-- PAGE: 6 -->

confirmed that information about self-service opening and how to register is available under the 'Join the Library' section of the online library catalogue. In addition, it explained that direct emails were sent to over 70,000 library users in 2017 to invite them to register for self-service opening, and that regular library newsletters are used to publicise the service.

**31.** In respect of the impact on library use, BC commented that library usage across the borough remains high, with 204,495 active users in December 2018. It states that while visitor numbers declined in 2017/18, following the implementation of the new library model, library visits increased by 4% in the first nine months of 2018/19 and visitor numbers are broadly consistent with the levels of visits prior to implementation of the new service. BC states that the increase in visitor numbers is particularly noticeable during self-service opening. It also states that self-service opening now accounts for around 30% of total library visits.

**32.** BC acknowledged that there has been a reduction in total physical book issues (6.2% fall in 2017/18), but states that this follows a wider regional and national trend. It noted that the fall in Barnet was less than the drop across London as a whole (a reduction of 7.2% for London Boroughs in 2017/18). BC also reported a positive increase in physical book issues (up by 7%) and digital issues (up by 27%) in the six month period from April to September 2018, as well as an overall increase in book issues per hour (up by 7% across all libraries).

**33.** BC confirmed that four Partnership libraries are now open and operational. It stated that all four libraries have extended their opening hours, offering between 20 and 42 opening hours per week (an average of 29 hours per week). It commented that in 2017/18, Partnership libraries recorded 91,489 visits and that this had increased to 110,637 visits for the first nine months of 2018/19.

**34.** Several of the representations state that the claim made by the Secretary of State in the minded to letter that "the total number of opening hours across the library network has increased to 904 hours per week" was inaccurate and that the true position is that total opening hours have fallen by 28%.

**35.** The representations state that the opening hours in the agreed 2016 model were not implemented as originally planned and that subsequent changes were made to the pattern of opening hours. The representations state that the changes mean that Core and Core Plus libraries are open for a combined total of 647.5 hours per week (comprising 187 staffed hours and 460.5 technology enabled opening hours). With 117 opening hours provided across the four Partnership libraries, the representations state that this gives a total of 764.5 weekly opening hours across the library service, and that this represents a shortfall of 139.5 hours against the planned 904 opening hours.

<!-- PAGE: 7 -->

**36.** BC acknowledges that the current opening hours are less than it previously envisaged, but notes that this still represents an increase in total weekly opening hours across the library network of 20% (up from 634.5 to 764.5 hours per week). It explained that the original intention was that technology enabled opening would be available from 7.00am until 10.00pm at Core and Core Plus libraries. However, it noted that due to planning and listed building constraints, and delays with the construction and handover of the new Colindale library, the implementation of technology enabled opening had to be deferred at some sites. BC further commented that following local consultation on the proposed pattern of opening hours, it was decided not to open Core and Core Plus libraries in the late evening, from 10.00pm to 11.00pm, but instead to use the budget to open Chipping Barnet and Hendon Core Plus libraries on a Sunday, and to maintain evening opening until 8.00pm across Core and Core Plus libraries.

**37.** BC also explained that security guards have continued in place in Core and Core Plus libraries during technology enabled opening, but that due to cost pressures, evening hours at Core libraries had to be reduced to 8.00pm (instead of 10.00pm) to fund their ongoing presence. BC noted that it has now agreed a new contract for security guard provision at Core Plus libraries from 6.00pm to 10.00pm and with a roving evening presence at the six Core libraries, and that it has written to all library users to advise them of the change and clarifying that their presence was to be gradually reduced on a library by library basis.

### Secretary of State's view

**38.** The Secretary of State notes the criticism regarding the claim in the minded to letter that opening hours had increased to 904 hours. The Secretary of State was aware that BC had made adjustments to the pattern of opening hours originally approved in April 2016, following consultation with users and local communities, and that technology enabled opening had yet to be fully implemented at all sites. The reference in the minded to letter was to the anticipated opening hours once the new library model was fully operational. The Secretary of State accepts that this could have been more clearly expressed.

**39.** The Secretary of State supports an approach which seeks to make the most efficient use of resources, while securing a comprehensive service. He notes that the introduction of technology enabled opening has enabled BC to maintain a physical library presence across the borough and to increase overall opening hours across the library network by 20%, offering access to library buildings and services at times not previously offered.

**40.** The Secretary of State further notes BC's explanation for the extended opening hours not having been implemented as originally planned, and that BC has sought to respond to local community feedback in determining the pattern of opening hours. He considers that it is for BC to determine the appropriate pattern of staffed and technology enabled opening hours across the library network, having regard to local needs and resources. The Secretary of State is satisfied that BC has demonstrated that it carefully considered this matter and that the current pattern of opening hours enables BC to provide a comprehensive and efficient service.

<!-- PAGE: 8 -->

**41.** That said, the Secretary of State considers that this does not impact upon his assessment of whether BC is providing a comprehensive and efficient service, based on the factors set out in the minded to letter. The Secretary of State remains of the view that BC continues to offer a comprehensive and efficient service.

## Impact on young people

**42.** Several of the representations comment on the impact of the reductions in staffed hours on young people and children. They assert that children under the age of 15 are excluded from using Core and Core Plus libraries during technology enabled opening unless accompanied by an adult, and that this has had a disproportionate and detrimental impact upon children and young people, particularly those from low income families, who rely on the library for study space, access to books and computers and a safe environment after school. They also claim that this policy is contrary to national policy guidance.

**43.** BC indicated that it carefully considered the age at which children can register for technology enabled opening. It confirmed that children aged 15 and over can register to use the library during technology enabled opening with parental consent, and that children under 15 are able to use the library during these times if accompanied by an adult registered for technology enabled opening. BC stated that this policy was adopted on child safety and safeguarding grounds, following advice from the Council's safeguarding lead, and that it reflects the approach adopted by other local authorities that have introduced technology enabled opening.

**44.** As noted in the minded to letter, BC commented that it planned the opening hours of Core and Core Plus libraries to ensure that all 10 libraries are staffed after school on weekdays until 5.00pm or 8.00pm, providing opportunities for children and young people to access the library unaccompanied.

**45.** The representations also claim that the libraries chosen as Partnership libraries are located in some of the most deprived areas in the borough, where there is a higher proportion of children and families on low incomes. In particular, they highlight Childs Hill library, which they state is located in an area with a high proportion of pupils eligible for free school meals and library members who are children in low income families. However, BC states it is one of the smallest

<!-- PAGE: 9 -->

libraries within Barnet, it has a low footfall, it has a lower number of visitors per hour than other libraries and is located within 1.5 miles of Golders Green and Cricklewood libraries. BC stated that Childs Hill was therefore considered suitable for transfer to a community partner.

**46.** In developing its library proposals BC was mindful of the need for the library service to support children and young people, particularly in disadvantaged areas. BC commented that it had regard to indices of multiple deprivation, child poverty and educational attainment data in developing the library model and in selecting the Core, Core Plus and Partnership libraries. BC stated that the four Core Plus libraries and six Core libraries are distributed across the borough, ensuring that all residents, including children and young people, have access to a fully staffed library within a reasonable distance.

**47.** In respect of the criticism that children in Childs Hill are unable to reach alternative staffed libraries, BC stated that Childs Hill is well served by public transport, with direct bus routes to Golders Green library (a Core library) and Finchley Church End library (a Core Plus library). It also noted that Childs Hill Partnership library is open for 20 hours per week over four days, including staffed sessions after school and on Saturdays.

**48.** A further criticism suggested that BC's reductions in stock have a disproportionate impact upon children and young people. BC stated that it continues to purchase new physical stock and that it has maintained its book fund at £400,000 per annum. It confirmed that stock is rotated across the Barnet library network, so that users are not limited to free access to books from their local branch. BC also confirmed that users have access to stock from other authorities through the London Libraries Consortium.

**49.** BC indicated that the online enquiry service, which can assist with information requests and access to digital resources, is available during both staffed and technology enabled opening. It confirmed that library users have free access to a wide range of digital resources, with users able to choose from thousands of fiction and non-

<!-- PAGE: 10 -->

fiction e-books & e-audio books, online encyclopaedia, back issues of newspapers and magazines, online courses, and reference material from home or within library buildings.

**50.** BC confirmed that all libraries have retained spaces designated for the specific use of children, teenagers and young adults and that study spaces have been retained across all Core and Core Plus libraries, providing over 400 study desks across the network.

**51.** The representations assert that the introduction of fines further compounds the barriers to library use for children and young people.

**52.** In respect of this criticism BC indicated that there are a number of ways in which users can avoid fines, including online renewals, email reminder notices and automated renewal of loans. BC confirmed that children's library cards do not incur overdue fines.

### Secretary of State's view

**53.** The Secretary of State considers that BC made clear the rationale for the selection of the Core, Core Plus and Partnership libraries. He is satisfied that the approach that BC carried out and the conclusions that it has reached are within the proper bounds of BC's discretion and that BC took reasonable steps to assess and mitigate the impact upon children and young people.

**54.** The Secretary of State notes that BC has set up the on-line enquiry line and made available digital resources, which provide access to library materials and support outside of staffed hours. He also notes that BC has retained designated areas for children and teenagers and study spaces across the library network.

**55.** The Secretary of State is satisfied that BC is continuing to provide study spaces for students and young people and that BC has responded positively to feedback by increasing study desk spaces where needed, including the addition of 30 extra study spaces at Colindale library and re-arranging the children's area at Golders Green library in response to feedback from library users.

<!-- PAGE: 11 -->

**56.** The Secretary of State notes that BC has sought to balance library opening hours with the needs of children and young people by ensuring that staffed opening hours at all 10 Core and Core Plus libraries are timetabled for after-school sessions on weekdays.

**57.** The Secretary of State considers that the schedule of library opening hours, alongside the provision of digital resources, Partnership libraries, mobile and home library services and the wide range of activities provided, enables BC to provide a comprehensive and efficient service for children and young people.

## Impact on other vulnerable groups

**58.** The representations also comment that the mitigation outlined in the EIA for older people and users with disabilities has not been implemented or has proved ineffective, leaving these users unable to access library services during technology enabled opening.

**59.** BC say that the mitigation for older people is largely complete, with the on-going provision of the mobile and home library service, computer training and digital support sessions, assistive technology across the library network, and designated quiet and study areas. BC noted that it has introduced a range of measures to assist disabled users, including accessible self-service kiosks, hearing loops, large-print and audio books, and accessible toilet facilities. It confirmed that staff and security guards have received disability awareness and safeguarding training.

**60.** BC considers that live CCTV supported by its existing emergency response arrangements provides an appropriate and effective security system during technology enabled opening. It confirmed that CCTV cameras are monitored in real time by the council's central monitoring station, with two-way audio communication into each library, and that an emergency phone is installed in all libraries with a direct connection to the monitoring station. BC also noted that it employs roving security to respond to any incident with the aim of a response time of 30 minutes.

<!-- PAGE: 12 -->

### Secretary of State's view

**61.** The Secretary of State notes that BC completed a risk assessment for each Core and Core Plus library before technology enabled opening went live. He further notes that BC undertook a detailed Equality Impact Assessment that included an analysis of the impact upon protected characteristic groups, including older people, disabled people, and young people. The EIA detailed how each of these groups would be affected and identified a range of mitigations to address potential adverse impacts.

**62.** He notes the suggestion that the user manual is not suitable for all users, but is satisfied that BC has made available a range of accessible guidance and support, including staff assistance during staffed hours, induction sessions, and telephone and online enquiry lines.

**63.** He notes the outstanding action to recruit and deploy volunteers to support older and vulnerable users, but considers that the range of mitigations implemented by BC demonstrates that it has given proper consideration to its equality duties.

## General

**64.** Other more general representations suggested that in interpreting the 1964 Act, the Secretary of State has set the bar for intervention too high and has failed to properly exercise his superintendence duty.

**65.** The Secretary of State notes the criticism that his assessment set out in the minded to letter was flawed. The Secretary of State considers that he has correctly directed himself as to the legal requirements under the 1964 Act and that he has applied the appropriate legal tests in reaching his decision.

**66.** The Secretary of State also notes the criticisms that the Save Barnet Libraries complaint was not handled fairly by the Department. He considers that the handling of this complaint has been consistent with the handling of other complaints that have been considered by the Department. He does not accept that the complaint has been treated unfairly and considers that there has been a rigorous and candid assessment of the information that has been provided to inform his decision.

<!-- PAGE: 13 -->

**67.** The Secretary of State further notes the criticism that he has not acted even-handedly in the treatment of the evidence. The criticism states he has not tested and assessed the evidence presented and has accepted detail provided by BC as unquestioned fact. The process of considering the complaint has required the careful analysis of the available information, including inviting further representations from interested parties and seeking further clarification from the Council, as appropriate, which is consistent with the Secretary of State's statutory duty of superintendence. The overarching question for the Secretary of State is whether the public library service provided by the local authority remains "comprehensive and efficient".

**68.** The Secretary of State notes the criticism about the departure from the Department's own national library policy in relating to the introduction and use of technology enabled opening, in particular, "that it was important that staffed hours meet the requirements of children and young adults who wish to visit the library unaccompanied". This detail relates to good practice guidance published by the Libraries Taskforce to help inform local authority chief executives and library portfolio holders considering introducing the use of technology to increase access to library buildings. The Secretary of State notes that BC undertook a detailed needs assessment to identify the Core, Core Plus and Partnership libraries. He further notes that BC considered how best to balance the needs of unaccompanied children, with the needs of other library users in order to develop a pattern of staffed hours within each locality and that BC timetabled staffed hours at libraries giving consideration to peak usage times, demographics of the local area, usage by targeted groups and opening hours across the network. He also understands that BC arranged staffed hours to offer a range of opening hours at each site across the week to ensure a spread of morning, afternoon and evening sessions are available within each locality and that BC timetabled staffed sessions for after school in each locality. The Secretary of State is of the view that BC has carefully considered this issue in designing its library service provision and that BC has been mindful of the published guidance.

**69.** The Secretary of State has also noted the views of previous High Court cases in particular the observations of Ouseley J in Bailey v London Borough of Brent [2011] EWHC 2572 (Admin) which commented, "Comprehensive has therefore been taken to mean delivering a service that is accessible to all residents using reasonable means, including digital technologies". He considers that the approach adopted by BC with the introduction of technology enabled opening is in line with that observation.

## Conclusions

**70.** Overall and after considering carefully all the factors and points that have been made by all parties, the Secretary of State does not consider there to be any serious doubt or uncertainty as to whether BC is complying with its legal obligations to provide a comprehensive and efficient library service. He is critical of some aspects of BC's communication of implementation of their library proposals, such as providing accurate timescales for: the extension of the online enquiry service; the implementation of extended evening opening hours at Core and Core Plus libraries; and retention of security guards at the Core and Core Plus libraries as well as the gradual reduction in their presence. The Secretary of State does acknowledge the importance of the library service to local residents, as well as the concerns expressed about the changes, however he is of the view that overall BC continues to offer a comprehensive and efficient service and that BC has given careful thought to ensuring that its library service continues to meet the needs of the community.

<!-- PAGE: 14 -->

**71.** The Secretary of State also considers that there is no other good reason why an inquiry should be ordered.

**72.** The Secretary of State recognises, however, that the duty under section 7 of the Act is a continuing duty, and he will continue to monitor BC's compliance with that duty in the same way as with any other library authority.
